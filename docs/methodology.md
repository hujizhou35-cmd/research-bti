# Development notes

Research BTI began with the author's experience leading competition teams: repeated responsibility shifting and missed defenses prompted an attempt to make team behavior easier to discuss.

## Four dimensions, sixteen fictional archetypes

The shared engine measures internal/external attribution (CX), responsibility tendency (OA), communication pattern (PR), and crisis response (TW). Each question has five value-keyed options, 0–4. Dimension strength is the sum divided by four times the question count, scaled to 0–10. Above 5 selects the first pole; 5 or below selects the second. A score within 0.75 of 5 is marked as a balanced tendency.

The quick mode uses the forty questions marked `priority: 1`; the full mode uses all eighty. Option order may reverse as a whole. The numeric value, not the screen position, determines the score. Type codes and scoring do not depend on language.

## AI-assisted editing

The author's retained development materials contain sixteen simulated behavioral profiles, three rounds of simulated answers, and a question-revision report. The report documents eighteen question rewrites, three topic changes and four softened extreme options. The simulations helped identify ambiguous situations, disciplinary assumptions, uneven answer gradients and socially obvious answers.

These are model-generated roleplays, not sixteen human participants or independent evidence of validity. Their agreement with intended profiles is not predictive accuracy. The original simulation files and editing drafts are retained privately; they are not part of the distributable application.

## Interpretation

Type names and illustrations are fictional and deliberately exaggerated. Scores describe answers to situations, not a diagnosis or final judgment of ability or character. Internal consistency alone cannot establish construct or predictive validity. Responsible use is voluntary self-reflection and discussion, rather than selecting or excluding people on the basis of a result.
